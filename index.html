What we do

Care that meets your child where they are

ABA breaks big goals into small, teachable steps and uses what motivates your child to make learning stick. We teach the skills that matter most to your family: communication, play, daily living, social connection and coping.

Center-based therapy

Structured, playful sessions in our therapy rooms, with peers nearby for social practice and a clinical team on site.

In-home therapy

Skills taught where daily life happens: morning routines, mealtimes, siblings, bedtime.

School and community

We work alongside teachers and in everyday places, from the playground to the grocery store, so new skills carry over.

Assessments

A BCBA completes a skills and behavior assessment and writes an individualized treatment plan with clear, measurable goals.

Parent and caregiver training

Families learn the same strategies we use, so progress continues between sessions and across settings.

BCBA supervision

Every technician is supervised by a Board Certified Behavior Analyst who reviews data, adjusts programs and meets with you regularly.

How it works

From referral to first session

Three steps, and our intake team stays in touch at every one of them.

  1. Referral and eligibility

    A parent, pediatrician, school or county case manager sends us a referral. We confirm the autism diagnosis and insurance, and request authorization from your county's community mental health program.

    Start with the online referral form or a phone call.

  2. Assessment and plan

    A BCBA meets your child and family, completes an assessment and writes an individualized treatment plan with the goals that matter most to you.

    Reviewed with you before therapy begins.

  3. Therapy and parent training

    A trained behavior technician works one-on-one with your child while the BCBA supervises, tracks progress and adjusts the plan. Parents train alongside us.

    Progress reviewed with you regularly; plans reassessed every six months.

For families

Questions parents ask us

Short answers here. For anything about your own child, our intake team is one call away.

Contact us
What is ABA therapy?

Applied Behavior Analysis is the most widely studied therapy for autism. It uses positive reinforcement to build communication, social, play, self-care and coping skills, and to reduce behaviors that get in the way of learning. Goals are individual, progress is measured session by session, and the plan changes as your child grows.

Does my child need a diagnosis first?

Yes. An autism spectrum diagnosis from a qualified provider such as a developmental pediatrician, psychologist or neurologist is required before ABA can be authorized. If you are still waiting on an evaluation, contact us and we will help you find one in your county.

How many hours a week will my child receive?

Hours are set by the BCBA's assessment and by the authorization from your county program, and they are reviewed with you before therapy starts. Younger children and children with more goals often receive more hours; the plan is adjusted as skills are mastered.

Where does therapy happen?

At one of our three centers, at your home, at school or out in the community, or a mix of these. The BCBA recommends settings based on your child's goals, and most families combine center time with home or school sessions.

What does a session look like?

A behavior technician works one-on-one with your child through play and everyday activities, following the programs in the treatment plan and recording data as they go. Sessions are active, warm and built around what your child enjoys. The BCBA observes sessions regularly and meets with you to go over progress.

How are parents involved?

Closely. Parent and caregiver training is part of every plan: you learn the strategies we use, practice them with your BCBA, and get support with routines at home. The skills that last are the ones the whole family uses.

What does it cost, and what insurance do you take?

We work with Medicaid through Washtenaw County CMH, DWIHN and St. Clair County CMH, with Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan, and with families who pay privately. ABA is a covered Medicaid benefit in Michigan, and for Medicaid members we bill your county program directly. For Blue Cross Blue Shield and other plans we check your benefits and explain any costs before services begin. Private pay families get a written plan and pricing up front.
